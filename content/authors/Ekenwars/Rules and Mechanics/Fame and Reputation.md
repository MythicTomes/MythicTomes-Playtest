## Fame and Reputation

Reputation measures how strongly a character, party, faction, or other entity is known for past deeds. It is a tool for tracking how the world reacts to the characters over time: who trusts them, who fears them, who remembers their victories, and who expects the worst from them.

A reputation can belong to one character or to the party as a whole. Track reputation individually when a deed, rumour, favour, or scandal belongs to one person. Track party reputation when the group acts together or when outsiders treat them as a single company. For example, a party slaying a dragon might gain reputation together, while one character winning a noble's favour might gain a separate reputation with that noble house.

Reputation only matters when it can reasonably affect the scene. An NPC must know, suspect, remember, or care about the reputation before it changes a roll or a DC.

### Reputation Scores, Fame, and Infamy

Reputation is measured from 0 to 10 reputation points. Track reputation points in the campaign tracker, then use the modifier bonus from the table when the DM decides that reputation affects a roll or DC.

| Reputation Points | Title             | Modifier Bonus |
| ----------------- | ----------------- | -------------- |
| 0                 | Unknown           | -              |
| 1                 | Known Face        | +1             |
| 2                 | Local Figure      | +1             |
| 3                 | Recognized Name   | +2             |
| 4                 | Notable Figure    | +2             |
| 5                 | Established Name  | +3             |
| 6                 | Widely Known      | +3             |
| 7                 | Regional Presence | +4             |
| 8                 | Regional Legend   | +4             |
| 9                 | Renowned Figure   | +5             |
| 10                | National Legend   | +6             |

**Fame** represents favourable or admirable recognition. It can come from public service, victories, honourable conduct, respected alliances, fulfilled promises, charity, patronage, or other deeds people speak of positively.

**Infamy** represents feared, suspicious, or hostile recognition. It can come from crimes, scandals, betrayal, cruelty, intimidation, dangerous power, dishonourable conduct, destructive accidents, or association with hated figures.

Fame and Infamy are not mutually exclusive. A character can have both for the same scope, and the same action can create different reputations among different groups. A party that saves a merchant company from bandits might gain Fame with that company, while gaining Infamy with the bandits or their allies.

Fame and Infamy are neutral modifiers. Fame is not always a bonus, and Infamy is not always a penalty. A famous hero may be trusted by villagers, but resented by rivals or targeted by enemies. An infamous criminal may be distrusted by guards, but respected by thieves or feared by debtors.

### Reputation Scope

Reputation is tracked by scope. A scope is the place, faction, region, institution, or social circle where the reputation applies.

Common scopes include settlements, districts, regions, noble houses, guilds, clans, temples, criminal groups, courts, armies, schools, professions, or the campaign overall.

Use the most specific relevant scope first. If no specific scope applies, the DM may use a broader reputation, such as a regional or overall score.

A scope can also borrow from a related reputation when news, politics, trade, kinship, authority, or shared danger connects two groups. The DM may use the full score, reduce it, or ignore it.

For example, if the party gains 3 Fame for saving a capital city from a dragon, nearby villages threatened by the same dragon might also treat the party as having 3 Fame. A distant village might treat that Fame as 1 because the story only reached them as rumour. A village that worshipped the dragon might instead record Infamy.

Another example: the party earns 1 Fame with a merchant guild for securing a trade route, but also earns 1 Infamy with the same guild after losing a substantial amount of cargo. Future guild interactions may be friendly, suspicious, or both, depending on which part of the story matters most to the NPC.

### Using Reputation

When a creature makes an ability check and reputation could reasonably affect the outcome, the DM may apply the modifier bonus for the relevant Fame or Infamy score. The DM chooses which reputation applies, whether it adds to or subtracts from the roll, and whether it modifies the roll or adjusts the DC.

Reputation most often affects Charisma checks, Wisdom (Insight) checks, and Intelligence (History) checks. It can affect other checks if the reputation changes the situation.

Examples:

- The party asks for lodging at a local tavern. The DM adds the party's Fame modifier bonus to a Charisma (Persuasion) check.
- A famous party member pretends to be a cult member in disguise. The DM might increase the Deception DC by the character's Fame modifier bonus, or add that bonus to a cultist's Wisdom (Insight) check to spot the disguise.
- An NPC tries to recall whether they have heard of the party. The DM may call for an Intelligence (History) check, applying the relevant Fame or Infamy modifier bonus if the reputation is public, recent, or local.

Some reputation exists only in backstory, distant regions, secret identities, or factions not yet active in the campaign. The DM does not need to track these scores until they can affect play. When a dormant reputation becomes relevant, assign a score and add it to the tracker.

### Earning and Losing Reputation

The DM awards or removes reputation when an event is notable enough to change how a community, faction, or region views the character or party. Reputation changes should usually have witnesses, evidence, official records, rumours, survivors, or someone with a reason to spread the story.

Reputation may also fade or lose relevance over time. A community might forget an old deed, a rumour might stop spreading, or a new crisis might overshadow the old story. Reputation can also weaken when the people who cared about it lose influence. For example, if a grateful king is succeeded by an indifferent heir, the party's Fame at court might be reduced or ignored by the new ruler's allies.

As a guideline, reward 1 point for a minor but memorable deed, favour, insult, arrest, rumour, or public incident. Award 2 points for a significant public deed, scandal, service, crime, victory, or betrayal. Award 3 points or more for a major event that changes local politics, saves or endangers many people, defeats a famous foe, exposes a faction, or becomes widely retold.

Fame and Infamy do not cancel each other unless the DM decides a later event changes how the older story is understood. A character might be both celebrated and distrusted for different reasons.

#### Sample Reputation Tracker

The following table shows one way to track reputation during a campaign. Each cell lists **Fame / Infamy reputation points**, not the modifier bonus. When a reputation affects a roll or DC, convert the relevant point value into its modifier bonus using the reputation table above. A blank cell is treated as 0 / 0. If only one side has a value, write the other side as 0, such as 2 / 0 or 0 / 1.

| Scope             | Party | Mara  | Kest  | Vell  | Orin  |
| ----------------- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Overall           | 1 / 1 | 2 / 0 | 0 / 1 |       | 1 / 0 |
| Greywatch         | 2 / 0 | 2 / 0 |       |       | 1 / 0 |
| Red Knife Gang    | 0 / 2 |       | 0 / 1 | 0 / 1 |       |
| Silvermere Temple |       | 1 / 0 |       | 2 / 0 |       |
| Crown Court       | 1 / 0 |       | 0 / 1 |       |       |

In this example, the party has 1 / 1 Overall reputation. They are known for one helpful deed and one public scandal, so NPCs who have only heard general rumours may react with mixed expectations. If either reputation applies, 1 reputation point gives a +1 modifier bonus.

Greywatch records the party as 2 / 0 because they saved caravan guards near the town. Mara also has 2 / 0 in Greywatch because witnesses remember her leading the rescue. Orin has 1 / 0 because he healed injured guards afterward. The party's 2 Fame still gives a +1 modifier bonus, but it shows stronger local recognition than a single point.

The Red Knife Gang records the party as 0 / 2 because the party disrupted gang business. Kest and Vell each have 0 / 1 because they were personally identified during the raid. Against gang members, this Infamy might help an Intimidation check, but it might hinder a Deception check if either character tries to pass unnoticed.

Silvermere Temple has no party reputation, but Mara has 1 / 0 for returning a relic and Vell has 2 / 0 for cleansing a shrine. If the party asks the temple for aid, the DM might use Vell's more specific reputation instead of the party's blank score.

Crown Court records the party as 1 / 0 because a magistrate received a favourable report. Kest has 0 / 1 because the same report names him as reckless. A courtier might admire the party while still distrusting Kest.

When reputation changes, note the source in a short changelog and update the table.

- **Session 3** - **Greywatch** _Party +2 Fame_ : saved caravan guards from raiders.
- **Session 4** - **Red Knife Gang** _Party +2 Infamy_ : ruined a smuggling route.
- **Session 5** - **Silvermere Temple** _Vell +2 Fame_ : cleansed the drowned shrine.

#### Sample Scope Hierarchy

Use separate tables when broad reputation exists alongside more specific faction or household reputations. The broadest scope gives the DM a default when no specific reputation applies, while narrower scopes show how particular groups remember the party differently.

##### National Reputation

| Scope                     | Party | Mara  | Kest  | Vell  | Orin |
| ------------------------- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ---- |
| The Empire                | 2 / 0 | 1 / 0 | 0 / 1 | 1 / 0 |      |
| Coalition of Noble Houses | 1 / 1 | 1 / 0 | 0 / 1 |       |      |

At the national level, the party is known for helping during a public crisis. Mara and Vell are more noteworthy because their roles in the crisis were visible.

The Coalition of Noble Houses is part of the Empire, so it may borrow from national reputation when court gossip spreads. It also keeps its own reputation score because the nobles have dealt with the party more directly. Mara has earned some goodwill with the coalition, while Kest has caused enough trouble to be remembered poorly.

The coalition table tracks how the alliance and its member houses judge the party. The coalition row is the overall reputation within the alliance. The house rows show how individual noble houses may agree with, soften, or strongly contradict that broader reputation. As a whole, the coalition may treat the party as useful but controversial, while House Sunspire is openly grateful and House Red Crane is hostile.
