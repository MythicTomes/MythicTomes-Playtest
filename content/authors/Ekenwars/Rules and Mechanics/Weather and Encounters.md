## Weather and Encounters

Use these rules when running wilderness travel, weather, and improvised travel encounters. Area-specific files, such as [[White Gate Travel]], provide the tables.

### Travel Day Procedure

1. Choose travel pace: slow, normal, or fast.
2. Roll the day's weather.
3. Roll the day's travel encounters, if you want random scenes for the journey.
4. Resolve hunting, foraging, navigation, or complications as needed.
5. At camp, resolve any weather effects and make Camp Rest saves.

Encounters do not need to be combat. They can be scenes, discoveries, hazards, social moments, hunting opportunities, or signs of nearby danger.

### Travel Pace

These rules streamline travel into encounter pacing instead of tracking exact distance.

| Pace   | Daily Encounters | Encounter Roll Modifier | Notes                                                                                                                 |
| ------ | ---------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Slow   | 5                | -2                      | Cautious but time-consuming. Use when the party scouts, hunts, searches, or protects supplies.                        |
| Normal | 3                | +0                      | The default pace.                                                                                                     |
| Fast   | 4                | +5, minimum result 5    | Quick but careless. Results above 10 count as 10, or the DM may roll again and add the second encounter to the first. |

Official SRD reference: [SRD 5.1](https://media.wizards.com/2016/downloads/DND/SRD-OGL_V5.1.pdf) says a fast pace makes characters "less perceptive," and its travel pace table gives a "-5 penalty to passive Wisdom (Perception) scores." [SRD 5.2](https://www.dndbeyond.com/srd) says fast pace imposes disadvantage on Wisdom (Perception or Survival) and Dexterity (Stealth) checks.

For this module, the fast pace minimum is 5 so hunting and other useful travel scenes remain possible.

### Weather and Camp Rest

Weather determines travel difficulty, visibility, resource pressure, and camp quality.

At the end of a wilderness travel day, each character makes a Constitution saving throw against the weather's Camp Rest DC. Add survival gear bonuses from [[Survival Gear]]. On a failure, the character gains a poor rest, or the DM may apply a lesser complication if the table calls for one.

Weather-imposed disadvantage on the Camp Rest save is cancelled if the character rests in any tier of tent or equivalent covered shelter.

### Encounter Use

Area encounter tables are optional tools for improvised travel. Use them when the party is travelling without a planned scene, when weather or pace creates extra pressure, or when you want the road to feel active.

For bandit or beast encounters, use the party's average level as the baseline:

- Easy encounter: 2 creatures, or 1d4 creatures
- Medium encounter: 4 creatures, or 1d4 + 1 creatures
- Hard encounter: 1 elite creature and 4 creatures, or 1d2 elite creatures and 1d4 + 2 creatures
- Challenging encounter: 1 elite creature and 8 creatures, or 1d2 elite creatures and 2d6 creatures

A regular creature has a CR of half or lower than the party's average level. An elite creature has a CR equal to or above the party's average level, but no more than 5 above it.

### Camp Events

Before or during a long rest, the DM may roll once on the area's camp event table. If the event becomes hostile, strenuous, or meaningfully disruptive, it counts as 1 hour of activity and the party must begin a new uninterrupted long rest.
