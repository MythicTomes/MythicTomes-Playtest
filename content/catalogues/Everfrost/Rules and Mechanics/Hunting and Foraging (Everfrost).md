## Hunting and Foraging

Adventurers traveling through the wilderness can hunt wild animals to supplement their food supplies. Hunting requires time, coordination, and knowledge of the environment. Unfavorable weather, dangerous terrain, and scarce wildlife can make hunting difficult or hazardous.

### Hunting Parties

A hunt requires **4 hours** of dedicated activity, during which participating creatures cannot travel or perform other activities.

Characters can form one or more hunting parties, each conducting an independent hunt. Each hunting party determines its own prey, makes its own checks, and receives its own food rewards. Characters cannot participate in multiple hunts simultaneously.

A hunting party can attempt up to four preparation checks, followed by one final hunting check. Each character can make only one preparation check per hunt, although a character who makes a preparation check can also perform the final hunting check.

### Determining Prey

At the beginning of each hunt, the DM rolls a d20 on the Hunting Prey table to determine the size of the prey encountered. The DM can instead select an appropriate result based on the location, available wildlife, and circumstances.

#### Hunting Prey

|  d20  | Prey Size | Base DC | Base Food Yield |
| :---: | :-------: | :-----: | :-------------: |
| 1–10  |   Small   |   10    |     1/2 lb.     |
| 11–17 |  Medium   |   13    |      1 lb.      |
| 18–20 |   Large   |   16    |      4 lb.      |

The prey's size determines the base DC for the hunt and the amount of edible food recovered on a successful hunt.

The DM determines the species of animal appropriate to the region. A prey's food yield represents the recoverable edible material, rather than its total body weight.

### Weather and Environmental Conditions

Weather and environmental hazards increase the difficulty of hunting. At the beginning of each adventuring day, the DM determines the prevailing weather conditions.

The weather modifier is added to the prey's base DC, establishing the **Hunting DC** for all checks made during the hunt.

##### Everfrost Weather Conditions

| Weather    | DC Modifier |
| :--------- | :---------- |
| Clear      | +0          |
| Harsh      | +3          |
| Extreme    | +5          |
| Disastrous | +8          |

For example, hunting Medium prey (base DC 13) during Extreme weather (+5) results in a Hunting DC of 18.

The DM can introduce additional modifiers for unusual circumstances, such as dangerous terrain, supernatural storms, unusually abundant wildlife, or depleted hunting grounds.

### Preparing the Hunt

Before attempting to capture or kill the prey, members of a hunting party can make preparation checks to improve their chances of success.

Each preparation check uses the Hunting DC. A successful check grants a **+1 bonus** to the final hunting check, while a failed check imposes a **−1 penalty**.

A hunting party can attempt each of the following checks only once per hunt:

- **Wisdom (Survival)**: Identify tracks, trails, and signs of animal activity.
- **Dexterity (Stealth)**: Approach the prey without being detected or alerting it.
- **Wisdom (Perception)**: Locate prey and identify opportunities to strike.
- **Intelligence (Nature)**: Apply knowledge of animal behavior, terrain, and environmental conditions.

Each preparation check must be made by a different character. A character cannot make more than one preparation check during the same hunt.

Preparation checks are optional. The hunting party can choose which checks to attempt and which characters perform them.

Add the bonuses and penalties from all preparation checks to determine the final **Hunting Preparation Modifier**. A failed preparation check does not immediately end the hunt or trigger a complication. Its penalty represents mistakes, wasted opportunities, or unfavorable positioning that make the final attempt more difficult.

### Resolving the Hunt

After completing preparation checks, the hunting party designates one character as the main hunter. The main hunter can be a character who previously made a preparation check.

The main hunter makes a Strength or Dexterity check against the Hunting DC, adding the Hunting Preparation Modifier. The hunter can add their Proficiency Bonus if they are proficient with the weapon or hunting equipment used to capture or kill the prey.

**Success.** If the total equals or exceeds the Hunting DC, the hunt succeeds. The hunting party obtains food according to the prey's size and the degree of success.

**Failure.** If the total is below the Hunting DC, the hunt fails and no food is obtained.

**Complication.** If the final check fails by 5 or more, the hunt fails and a complication occurs. The DM determines the complication, either by choosing an appropriate event or rolling on a hunting complication table.

### Food Yield

On a successful hunt, the party obtains the prey's base food yield, plus 1/2 pound of additional food for each point by which the final hunting check exceeds the Hunting DC.

The total food recovered cannot exceed twice the prey's base food yield.

#### Food Yield by Prey Size

| Prey Size | Base Yield | Maximum Yield |
| --------- | ---------- | ------------- |
| Small     | 1/2 lb.    | 1 lb.         |
| Medium    | 1 lb.      | 2 lb.         |
| Large     | 4 lb.      | 8 lb.         |

For example, a hunting party successfully hunts Large prey with a Hunting DC of 21. The main hunter's final check totals 26, exceeding the DC by 5. The hunt would yield 4 + 2 1/2 = 6 1/2 pounds of food.

If the check totaled 30, the calculated yield would exceed the prey's maximum of 8 pounds. The party instead recovers 8 pounds.

Each pound of food can sustain one Medium creature for one day or satisfy its food requirement for a Long Rest.

The DM can adjust the food recovered to reflect the nature of the prey, particularly unusual creatures or animals with exceptionally little or abundant edible material.

### Hunting Complications

When the final hunting check fails by 5 or more, the hunting party encounters a complication. Complications represent the dangers of pursuing wildlife through hostile wilderness. They can involve the prey itself, other creatures, environmental hazards, or the loss of valuable supplies.

#### Hunting Complications

| d8  | Complication                                                                                                          |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Predator.** A hostile creature is attracted by the hunters' activity.                                               |
| 2   | **Hostile Prey.** The prey becomes aggressive and attacks the hunting party.                                          |
| 3   | **Hazardous Terrain.** The hunters encounter thin ice, unstable ground, a ravine, or another environmental hazard.    |
| 4   | **Lost Equipment.** A weapon, ammunition, or a piece of hunting equipment is lost or damaged.                         |
| 5   | **Sudden Weather.** A localized storm or dangerous change in weather threatens the hunters.                           |
| 6   | **Lost Trail.** The hunters become disoriented and must navigate back to their original location.                     |
| 7   | **Unexpected Encounter.** The hunters encounter another traveler, creature, or wilderness event determined by the DM. |
| 8   | **Rival Hunters.** Other hunting parties notice and attempt to steal each other's prey                                |

A complication does not necessarily result in combat. The DM determines its consequences and resolves any necessary ability checks, saving throws, or encounters.

If a complication interrupts the hunt, the attempt ends in failure. A new hunt requires another 4 hours and a new set of checks.

### Multiple Hunts

A party can divide its members into separate hunting parties to increase the number of simultaneous hunting attempts.

Each hunting party independently determines its prey, performs preparation checks, and resolves its final hunting check. A character cannot contribute to another hunting party's checks while participating in their own hunt.

Splitting the party allows more opportunities to obtain food, but reduces the number of characters available to assist each hunt and exposes the groups to separate complications.

**Dividing the Party.** Six adventurers divide into two hunting parties of three. Each group hunts for 4 hours. Each hunting party can make up to three preparation checks, since each member can attempt only one. Both parties then make their own final hunting checks, potentially recovering food from two separate animals.
